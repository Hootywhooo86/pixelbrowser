# Bit Browser privacy policy

_Last updated: [DATE]_

Bit Browser is a web browser for Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac and Linux, made by [YOUR NAME OR COMPANY] as part of the 128bit family of apps. This policy explains what Bit Browser does with your information. The short version: **we don't collect it.**

## What we collect

**Nothing.** Bit Browser has no account to sign up for, no analytics, no advertising IDs and no tracking. We don't run servers that receive your browsing, and we can't see what you do in the app.

## What stays on your device

These are stored only on your phone or computer:

- Your history, open and pinned tabs, bookmarks, downloads and settings.
- **Saved passwords, addresses and payment cards** (Bit Password Manager). They're encrypted with your device's secure storage (Android Keystore, iOS Keychain or your computer's credential store). Viewing them needs your fingerprint, face or device PIN where your device supports it.
- What you ask Bitter to remember, and Bitter's activity log.
- XP, streaks, badges and quests.
- Counts of blocked ads and trackers.
- The crash log, if you turn it on (see below).

You can see and delete all of it in **Settings → Shields & privacy → Your data**.

## The AI assistant (Bitter)

- Bitter only works with an AI provider **you choose** (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or a model running on your own computer), using **your own** API key. Requests go **directly** from your device to that provider. They never pass through us.
- Bitter only sends a page, your question, or the tabs you pick when you ask it to. What the provider does with that is covered by **their** privacy policy.
- Bitter is off in private tabs.
- **AI slider off:** nothing at all is sent to any AI.
- Your API keys are stored in your device's secure storage.

## Sync (optional)

If you turn on sync, your devices share history, tabs, bookmarks, settings, Bitter's memory and, only if you choose, passwords, addresses and cards. Everything is **encrypted on your device** with a key made from your 24-word sync code before it's uploaded. The sync server stores blobs it **cannot read**. It never receives your sync code or your key. Leaving sync deletes your copy on the server.

## Bit Shields and phishing protection

- Ad and tracker filter lists (EasyList, EasyPrivacy, uBlock filters and others) are downloaded straight from the people who publish them.
- Lists of dangerous sites (Phishing Army, URLhaus) are downloaded the same way, and every check happens **on your device**. The addresses you visit are never sent anywhere to be checked.
- These downloads only tell the list publishers that a copy of the list was requested, like any website visit.

## Crash reports (optional, off by default)

If you turn crash reports on, Bit Browser keeps a short log of errors **on your device**. It's only sent if you choose to share it (for example by email), and you can read it first.

## Permissions

- **Camera:** to scan the sync QR code, and for websites you allow (video calls).
- **Microphone:** only for websites you allow.
- **Location:** only for websites you allow.
- **Notifications:** only for websites you allow.
- **Install apps (Android):** so an APK you downloaded can be opened.
- **Biometrics:** to unlock saved passwords and private tabs.

Websites ask each time, and you can change your answers in **Settings → Site settings**.

## Websites you visit

Websites you open see what any browser sends them (your IP address, the pages you request and any cookies they set). Their privacy policies apply. Bit Shields blocks many trackers, and private tabs forget everything when you close them.

## The 128bit family

Sharing activity with a 128bit account is **off** until you connect an account and turn sharing on for each kind of event. 128bit accounts aren't available yet.

## Children

Bit Browser isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anything from anyone, including children.
